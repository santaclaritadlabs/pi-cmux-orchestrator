# Code of Conduct

## Our Commitment

We want `pi-cmux-orchestrator` to be a professional, constructive, and welcoming open-source project.

Participants are expected to interact respectfully regardless of experience level, background, identity, employer, technical preferences, or choice of tools and AI providers.

## Expected Behavior

Examples of positive behavior include:

- discussing technical disagreements based on evidence;
- giving actionable and respectful feedback;
- acknowledging tradeoffs and uncertainty;
- accepting corrections;
- documenting security concerns responsibly;
- respecting the time and attention of maintainers and contributors;
- helping newcomers understand project conventions;
- attributing the work of others appropriately.

## Unacceptable Behavior

The following behavior is not acceptable:

- harassment, threats, intimidation, or personal attacks;
- discriminatory or derogatory comments;
- deliberate disruption of discussions or project infrastructure;
- publishing another person's private information without permission;
- knowingly submitting malicious code, dependencies, payloads, prompts, fixtures, or repositories;
- deliberately attempting to expose contributor or maintainer credentials;
- using security reports as a means of coercion or harassment;
- intentionally concealing supply-chain or execution risks in a contribution;
- repeatedly ignoring reasonable maintainer requests relating to security or community safety.

Technical criticism is welcome. Personal attacks are not.

## Security Research

Good-faith security research is welcome.

Testing that could affect systems, accounts, infrastructure, credentials, or data belonging to other people requires prior authorization.

Security vulnerabilities should be reported according to `SECURITY.md` rather than disclosed through public issues.

## Enforcement

Project maintainers are responsible for interpreting and enforcing this Code of Conduct.

Responses may include:

- a private warning;
- removal or editing of inappropriate content;
- temporary participation restrictions;
- rejection or closure of contributions;
- temporary or permanent exclusion from project spaces.

Enforcement decisions should be proportionate to the behavior, its impact, and whether it is repeated.

## Reporting Conduct Issues

Conduct concerns may be reported privately to:

`opensource@santaclaritadlabs.com`

When the issue itself is a software vulnerability, follow `SECURITY.md` instead.

Reports will be handled as privately as reasonably possible.

## Scope

This Code of Conduct applies to project-controlled spaces and when someone is acting as an official representative of the project.
